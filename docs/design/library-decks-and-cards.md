# Library, decks and cards

The web deck page starts with two panels. The first shows cards available for review, today’s goal status and Review. This count includes eligible new cards as well as scheduled cards. With nothing available, show the next due date and Add card. The second panel shows total, New, Learning and Known counts with state icons. At narrow widths the total gets its own row above the three states. These counts cover the whole deck, regardless of what is due today.

## The card list

Show cards as rows in a bordered panel: state icon, term and meaning. Set forms after the first “ · ” in `text-2`. Limit the row to three term lines and two meaning lines; the open card shows the full content. Below 36 rem of list width, place the meaning below the term. Keep AI badges on the open card rather than repeating them down the list. Important cards show a filled `amber-mark` star after the term and include Important in the accessible name.

Place Filter and Sort above the list, with search beside them on desktop and `/` as its shortcut. Narrow web layouts put search in the top bar. Filter opens a two-level menu: fields, then values for one field. Show active-value counts beside fields and matching-card counts beside values, accounting for other active filters. Keep the menu narrow and reserve an icon or check slot in every row. Highlight either the pointer row or keyboard focus, and let directional arrows open a field or return to the field list.

Filter fields are State, Due, Section, Tags and Important. Due is a single choice: Any time, Today or This week. Section lists No section last; Tags lists most-used tags first. Important toggles directly on the first level. Hide Section, Tags and Important when there is no relevant choice. Combine fields with AND and values within a field with OR, matching API search. `packages/client/src/deck-filters.ts` defines the fields for client rendering.

The Filter button counts active fields. Each active field also gets a removable chip with its icon, name and chosen values. Its label reopens that field; its ✕ clears it. Keep the value menu open until dismissed, even after its last value is cleared. Show Clear filters when at least two fields are active. Remove selections that refer to tags or sections no longer present.

Sort offers Section when sections exist, When it’s back, Recently added and A–Z. Section is the default for sectioned decks: deck section order, oldest card first, unsectioned cards last. Otherwise Recently added is the default and groups by date. When it’s back groups Due now, This week, Later and Not started, and alone shows row dates. A–Z is ungrouped. Filters and sort reset when leaving the visit. To review filtered results, use Select cards, then Select all and a review action.

## Sections

Each section has a heading and card count. For sequential progression, the review panel shows the current section, the number of cards needed to open the next, a Known progress track and Go to. Go to clears filters and scrolls to the section, whose heading has a green You are here label. Manual progression offers Start once ready; it is primary only when nothing is due. Automatic progression opens the next section on the qualifying grade. Locked sections remain readable with muted headings and rows, and a lock replaces the state icon on unstarted cards. Ready sections show Start on `plate-2`. Start anyway opens a later section early, with confirmation when earlier locked sections must also open. Put it first in the section menu where one exists, otherwise beside the heading. Section contents do not collapse.

The owner moves cards from the list and arranges sections in deck settings. A section's heading has a menu with Add card here, Rename and Arrange sections; the deck's menu adds New section, and every learner's menu has Select cards. Every learner's menu on a section with cards due today starts with **Review this section**, a review of that section alone, so a lesson can be refreshed ahead of a backlog; the count is the section's own share of the deck's due count, so a section not open yet offers it once a card there has started. It runs to the section's end like a deck review, and a member’s menu also supports reporting content, but no editing actions.

A card drags onto any section, with a long press on touch; the lifted copy is the row itself on an `edge-2` plate at 1.02×, and it goes the moment it drops, because the card is already in its new place. A section with no cards shows a dashed well to drop into.

Change section order in deck settings rather than by dragging the deck page’s sections. Web settings use arrows with rename, archive and New section; phone settings use handles after Edit. Show Archived sections when restoration is available. How sections open offers automatic sequence, manual sequence or all at once. Reordering applies immediately with Undo. Archiving a section first asks whether to keep its cards unsectioned or archive them too, then offers Undo. The phone asks through a sheet with Keep the cards and Archive its cards too.

On the phone the deck's summary shows sections as tiles instead of the review panel's block ([section pages](../proposals/section-pages.md)). A row of 116 pt tiles scrolls sideways under the summary's padding and opens centred on the learner's section, or, once every section is open, on the first one not fully known. A tile holds the section's ring, filled with known and then learning and closing into a check when every card is known, its number, its name on up to two lines, and known of total; the learner's tile is a plate ringed in ink, a waiting tile is dashed with a lock in its ring and its card count, and cards with no section get a last tile named No section. Under the tiles, one sentence says what opens the next section, with a meter and, once it is ready, Start. The plate ends on **All sections**, a row with the section count that opens the Sections page.

The Sections page (`library/[deck]/sections`) is for everyone studying the deck. A plate first shows how many sections are fully known of how many, the count to review, a column per section filled with known and learning, dashed while it waits, with a short ink bar under the learner's, and the sentence about what opens next. Under it the sections are the path a learner walks: each stop's number sits in its ring, a waiting stop's ring is dashed with a lock beside it, the second line says known of total, the learner's stop is tinted, and a tap opens the section's page. Cards with no section follow the path as No section. The owner also gets Edit, a swipe to Rename and Archive at rest, New section as the path's next stop, How sections open and Archived sections; a member sees How sections open as text. A tile, a heading, Previous and Next and a stop all open a section's page: the deck's page kept to one section, with Section N of M, percent known, the state bar, the sentence when it concerns this section or Opens after the one before, Review this section or Start anyway, Filter and Sort, and the section's cards, dimmed while it waits. Its ⋯ holds the section's rows in the heading's order. The lock on a card that has not come up and the owner's Add card here work as on the web; on the phone Add card here opens capture with that section chosen.

## Select cards

Select cards swaps each row's state icon for a checkbox of the same 15 px, so choosing moves nothing in the list; shift-click selects a range. While choosing, the row that holds Filter and Sort becomes the selection toolbar and sticks to the top of the page: ✕ to stop, the count, Select all or Clear, and one amber **Actions** menu, so the bar stays calm however many things can be done. It covers nothing, so the phone's tab bar stays, and Escape leaves unless a menu or dialog is open.

The selection Actions menu starts with Review N due and Review all N. Review all includes started cards not reviewed today, due cards first, then scheduled order. New cards start through the day’s draw. Next offer Mark important, or Unmark important if all selected cards are marked. Members can use review and importance actions. Owner-only actions follow a separator: Tags…, Move to section…, Move to deck… when another owned deck exists, then Archive in red.

Mark, Move to deck and Archive act at once and offer Undo, as on the phone. On the web, Mark, Tags… and Move to section… leave the cards chosen, so the next change needs no choosing again, as Gmail keeps threads ticked after a label; Move to deck and Archive end the selection, since the cards leave the list. A chosen card the list stops showing, such as one unmarked under the Important filter, leaves the selection, so the count names only cards in sight. Each change's toast stacks over the last, so an earlier Undo stays in reach.

Filtering and then Select all is how a learner reviews a tag or a search, such as every food card the night before a class. The review is a round: it ends on Round done, and Continue goes on to the day's draw. One review takes at most 200 cards.

Tags… opens a dialog listing the tags the chosen cards carry, then the learner's other tags, each with a checkbox and "2 of 3": a tag on every card is ticked, one on some is mixed, and a press puts it on every card or takes it off every card. A search field above the list finds a tag in any case and offers Create for a new name, which arrives ticked. Save writes only the cards that change, a card already holding twenty tags is left without the new one and the toast says so, and Undo puts every card's tags back.

On the phone, Select cards is in the deck's menu for any deck with cards in it, a member's included. The selection toolbar floats as a glass bar above the tab bar, as Photos and Mail pin theirs: ✕, the count, Select all, and an amber ⋯ that opens iOS's own menu of what can be done to the chosen cards. Its first group is the same **Review 2 due** and **Review all 5** as the web's, each hidden when it would take no cards, and the review ends on Round done. Next, **Mark important** marks every chosen card, or **Unmark important** once every chosen card is marked; the mark is the learner's own, so a member has it too. Under a separator, the owner's Tags, Move to section, whose sheet also offers New section to name one and move the cards into it, Move to deck when the learner has another deck, and Archive. Tags opens a sheet that saves with the web's dialog inside it: a field that finds a tag or offers Create, then the chosen cards' tags and the learner's others, each a row with a check on every card, a dash on some, and "2 of 3" at its end; a tap cycles it as the web's checkbox does, and ✓ writes only the cards that change. Each change acts at once and offers Undo, which gives every card back what it had, and ends the selection, as iOS's Mail leaves Edit after Mark or Move. Filter and Sort dim while choosing, so what is chosen never changes under a filter.

## The open card

A card opens where it can be read with the list. When the page is wide enough for a 640 px list and the 400 px card, which is a 1280 px window beside the rail, the card sits beside the list on a plate, in its own sticky scroll. When it is not, the card is a side sheet on the same plate at the end edge over a dimmed page on a desktop, and a drawer over the whole screen on touch. The shape follows the window, so a resize moves an open card between beside and sheet; it waits while a field is being edited or the Move sheet is open, so the switch never drops what is in progress. J and K walk the list in its sorted order, Escape closes the card, except while a field is being edited, when it only cancels the edit, and the open card lives in `?card=`, so Back closes it.

## Deck menu and settings

Deck actions live behind one menu: Deck settings opens a screen, and the name is changed there rather than inline, Export offers an Anki package, a Lymi file or a spreadsheet ([imports.md](imports.md)), Archive leaves the deck list with an Undo toast. Cards archive the same way, from the row.

The **AI** group in deck settings is the owner's alone: one card holding a checkbox per field the AI may fill (Meaning, Example, Example translation, Notes, Pronunciation) and an optional **Instructions** box. A checkbox saves when pressed and the instructions save when the field is left, like the rest of the screen. The fields start from the default for the deck's language. A member's About this deck shows neither.

Members get About this deck, Report and Leave deck rather than owner settings, Add, Export or Archive. Card menus retain personal actions such as Mark important and Report. Name the owner in the deck subline and About this deck, which shows description, language, review modes and card count as read-only facts. Leaving requires confirmation because rejoining depends on a still-available join link or publication. State that reviews are retained; the safe action is Keep studying.

On the web, a deck is made in one sheet: its name in the standard input, its purpose as the form's one group of radio cards, its language when the purpose is language learning, and how it is asked in a captioned Select. The controls follow [forms.md](system/forms.md), without a larger cut for the name. Creating it lands the learner in the empty deck, which is where the cards go next. Everything chosen there is changeable afterwards.

Deck settings use `/library/$deckId/settings`. Review modes offers three radio rows: recognition, production or both, previewed with a real card. `directions` is the legacy storage and API name. Selection uses a neutral border and filled dot. Choices save immediately; names save on blur or exit. Show Saved in the header rather than a Save button. [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-a-decks-direction-is-a-filter-not-a-migration.md) defines what mode changes do to existing progress.

## Tags

The owner's deck settings carry a **Tags** group: one row naming how many tags the deck's cards carry, with Manage, that opens the deck's Tags page, `/library/$deckId/settings/tags`, with Settings as its back link. The page has a search field, then In use, each tag with its card count and a ⋯ menu of Rename…, Merge into… and Archive, then Archived tags, each with Restore. Every change stays in this deck, because a tag is the learner's across decks and a deck's settings should not reach into others: Rename retags this deck's cards, and renaming onto a name the learner already uses merges the two, which the field says before it happens and the button reads Merge; Merge into… picks one of the deck's other tags. A change of case alone respells the tag everywhere, since one name is one tag. Archive takes the tag off this deck's cards and offers Undo; Restore puts it back on the same cards in the same place, except a card that has filled its twenty since. A rename offers Undo, and a merge does not, because the merged cards can no longer be told apart. A member sees no Tags group: the owner's tags are theirs to filter by, not to change.

On the phone, the owner's deck settings carry a Tags row beside Sections, naming how many tags the deck's cards carry, and it pushes the same page, each row's commands in its ⋯ and as VoiceOver actions. There merging is always its own action: Rename refuses a name already on this deck's cards and points to Merge into…, and Merge into… asks in iOS's alert, naming both tags and that a merge can't be undone, with Keep both as the safe button. A name used only in other decks is a plain rename with Undo, since it joins the tag of that name. Past fifteen tags, Find a tag in the bar opens the phone's search over the same rows. The web still merges on rename until it follows.

## Review card and pictures

A review card's top line names where the card came from: the deck with its book icon, then the mode, and the state chip at the end. A review can draw from every deck, so the deck is always there; the card's language code follows the mode only when it differs from the deck's.

A card's picture is content, not decoration, so it follows the term's rules. In a picture mode it is the cue, on the start edge like the term, up to 30% of the viewport or 240 px tall, and it keeps that size on reveal while the target and the other text field rise in under the rule. In a text mode the picture joins the context under the target at up to 120 px. The card page shows it at up to 140 px on its own plate, with the description wrapped to the picture's width under it, so the two read as one thing. `CardPicture` holds the picture's own shape from the first frame over a `plate-2` well, fades it in with `enter-fade`, and then drops the well, because a road sign on transparency would otherwise sit in a grey box. When it cannot load it says "Couldn't load the picture" and shows the description in its place, so the card can still be revealed and graded. The card page says in words when picture review is waiting for a picture or a description.

Pictures are kept for offline review in a Cache Storage bucket per learner, and every other learner's bucket is dropped, so a shared browser never shows one learner's pictures to another. The picture's URL carries its id, so a replaced picture is a new entry, and the server sends `private, no-store`, so the HTTP cache holds nothing. When Cache Storage refuses, the picture still loads, it just is not kept offline.

## Library

Library lists the decks without a series first, as one plate of rows with no heading, because they are the learner's own decks. Each series follows under its own heading: its name and a menu with New deck, Edit series, Move up, Move down and Delete series, where New deck makes the deck inside that series. On the phone the menu has Choose decks, Rename series, Reorder series once there are two, and Delete series; Reorder series opens a sheet of every series that drags by its handles and saves as each drops. A long name truncates rather than pushing the menu under it.

The series' decks follow in one plate in the series' order, and while any is due **Review all** sits under the plate: a strip in the due count's `amber-soft`, as wide as the plate and tucked beneath its bottom edge so the plate rests on it, with the series' due count and a chevron in the same columns as its rows' counts and chevrons, that reviews every deck in the series. It is a tint, never the primary amber, so Today's Review stays the one primary, and it steps aside when nothing is due.

While a deck is dragged over a series, its plate fills with `plate-2`. The learner's own decks can be dragged into, out of and along a series; a long press starts the drag on touch, Space on the keyboard, and Enter still opens the deck. A deck without a series keeps its place by creation date, so the loose list takes a drop without making room, and when every deck is in a series a dashed target appears below them only while a deck is dragged, as the way out of one. The arrow keys can carry a deck out of its series as well as along it.

The same moves exist without dragging: **New series** in Library's menu, **Edit series** (a name and a checklist of decks with up and down arrows), and **Move to series** in a deck's menu. Deleting a series always asks, because it is the one write with no Undo: the series is gone for good, and when it holds decks the learner also chooses whether they are archived with it or stay loose in Library. Everything else about a series undoes with a toast.

A deck in Library is a row in its group's plate, drawn like Today's deck rows, because Library is for choosing a deck to open: the whole row is the link and ends in Go. The first line is the name, which inside a series drops the series' own name and the separator after it, so "Eesti A1 · Words" reads "Words"; the second is the deck's language when it has one, the card count, and, with nothing due, when the next card is back. The due count sits just before Go's chevron while cards are due, so the two read as one end of the row. A joined deck adds its owner on a third line: a deck added from Explore draws [the publisher's mark](explore.md#publisher-identity) there, and a deck joined through a private link keeps the learner plate, because only publishing makes a photo public. The split between states is the deck's progress, not something to choose by, so it lives on the deck page and the row carries no stripe: due is its only colour, and a page of decks with nothing due stays quiet. A row of new cards names its actor, because a card an integration wrote must never look like one the learner typed.

**Search** and **New deck** sit beside Library's menu on a desktop, as icon buttons. Search opens into a field there with an X inside that closes it, and an empty field closes on its own when focus leaves it. On the phone, where the title has no room beside it, the field and a New deck button share a row under the title. Search narrows Library as the learner types, by a deck's name, its language or its series' name: a series none of whose decks match steps aside, nothing can be dragged until the search is cleared, and with nothing left Library says so and offers Clear search. `/` reaches it from anywhere on the screen. Library ends with its last group. Archived decks sits in Library's menu while any deck is archived, because deleting a series can archive its decks; otherwise Archived is reached from You.

## Adding and editing a card

A card is added and changed with one form, `CardForm`, so adding and editing use the same field controls. Term, meaning and deck come first, because capture during a lesson needs nothing else. The deck is the one the sheet was opened from, or the deck last added to.

Optional fields open from a horizontally scrolling `FieldChipRow` below the deck, with icons, labels and overflow fades. Text chips append and focus a field; once shown, it stays until the next card even if cleared. Choice chips open a desktop panel or touch drawer and then show the selected name or count. Section appears first when available, review modes follow the content fields, and field chips are ordered by use in the deck or its enrichment defaults when empty. Hover reveals names behind a count. Meaning, example and hint grow from one line; notes start taller. Adding follows the deck language and offers Hint last. Editing shows all existing fields and also offers language. Validation reveals and focuses any invalid field.

Once an example has text, show its translation in a `plate-2` area below a divider, with a translate icon, `text-2` and its own text direction. The fields share hover and focus treatment; the translation has its own AI badge. An empty translation explains whether AI will fill it after save or offers manual entry. Editing an AI-translated example clears its old translation; a manual translation stays with a message that it belongs to an earlier example. Examples have a 250-character display limit excluding readings and stress marks; existing over-limit examples can be saved unchanged.

Adding closes the sheet and says "Added" in a toast. "Create more", a checkbox beside the action and remembered per learner, keeps the sheet open instead: "Added" or "is already in" then appears under the term, and language, source and tags stay for the next card. ⌘ Enter adds from any field. A drawer has no Cancel, because a swipe or a tap outside puts it away. Closing a sheet that holds anything typed says "Card discarded" with Undo, which brings the draft back, picture included; closing an edit with unsaved changes says "Changes discarded" the same way. A picture is sent after the card exists, since the picture routes need its id; if it fails the card is still added, the message says so, and its Add picture opens that card's edit sheet at the picture field.

The card page reads as a page. Marking a card important is the first item of its menu, Mark important or Unmark important, offered to the owner and a member alike because the mark is their own; a member’s menu also supports reporting content, but no editing actions. The bar gets no button for it, because the bar is for reading and moving between cards. A marked card's meta line under the term starts with the amber star and the word Important, so the state is never the colour alone. Review's card menu offers the same item to anyone. Nothing on it is edited in place, because a page where any text can turn into a field is a page where a tap to read becomes an edit. The pencil in its bar opens the same form, filled in, and Save writes only what changed. A description that names the term or the meaning is refused before anything is sent, by the same rule the server applies.

On the card page, place an AI badge beside each enriched field’s label, or below the term for pronunciation. Put the example’s play control at the far edge of its first line and the translation below in `muted`. One badge can cover an AI-written example and translation; if only the translation is AI-written, badge that translation alone. Show a short shimmer while translating. Only the owner sees a stale translation and its warning. Failed translation shows Couldn’t translate the example with Try again.

While `enrichmentStatus` is `working`, show two shimmer lines for missing Meaning, Example and Notes, one for pronunciation and a short placeholder for language. Poll normal card reads every two seconds. Stop each placeholder when its field is filled or skipped, and stop all on failure. Keep the card’s existing content visible. Waiting itself adds no AI badge or list-row status.

Offer Enrich in the owner’s card menu while the language or an allowed field is missing. Members cannot request it. A failed card shows Couldn’t enrich this card with Try again on the card page, without repeating the failure in list rows or capture. A retry sets `working` as soon as the request returns. Reject a request with nothing eligible to fill rather than reporting an empty success.

## Notes

A card's notes are Markdown source in a small subset, so a breakdown such as *hea aeg* → *head aega* reads clearly in review and on the card page. The subset is paragraphs, line breaks, `**bold**` or `__bold__`, `*italic*` or `_italic_`, `++underline++`, `~~strikethrough~~`, links (`[text](https://example.com)` or `<https://example.com>`, with `http`, `https` or `mailto` destinations), bulleted lists (`-`, `+` or `*`) and numbered lists (`1.` or `1)`), with a backslash to keep a marker literal. A single line break stays a line break, so a plain note written before formatting reads as it did. Everything else, including headings, images, tables, code and raw HTML, shows as the text that was typed, and nothing in a note is ever rendered as HTML. `parseNotes` in `packages/core/src/notes.ts` is the one reader, so the app, and later the public catalog, render the same thing.

Review's answer side and the card page render the formatting in the size and colour of the notes around it, with list markers in `muted`. Links have an underline, a visible keyboard focus ring and open in a new tab so following one keeps the current review; relative destinations and other schemes stay literal text. Wherever notes are quoted inside a sentence, such as the Activity line "Notes changed to …", or matched by search, only the words count, through `notesToText`. The API, MCP and CSV export carry the source unchanged, and the 2,000-character limit counts the source. The card form's notes stay a plain textarea with no hint, toolbar or preview: the syntax is ordinary Markdown, learned by using it. Term, meaning, example, deck descriptions and imported notes stay plain text.

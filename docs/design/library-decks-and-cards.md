# Library, decks and cards

A deck opens on two stacked plates, because one is an action and the other describes the deck. Today's plate comes first and is the one with an action: the cards to review now, which counts the new cards the draw can hand out as well as scheduled reviews, so it never says "due" of a card that has no schedule yet, the line the streak card uses for where today's goal stands, and Review. With nothing due it shows when the next card is back and Add card where Review was. The deck's plate under it is four columns with hairlines between: the total, then New, Learning and Known, each count beside its state's icon. A narrow plate cannot fit four counts in the hundreds, so there the total takes its own row and the three states sit under it. The counts are the whole deck, so they never read as zero on a quiet day. There are no tabs until a second one has content to hold.

A deck's words read as a glossary, because the list is for reading: an integration writes and tidies most cards, and a member of a shared deck cannot edit. Each word is a row of one plate with hairlines between: the state's icon, the term, and its meaning beside it. When a term lists its forms joined by " · ", the forms after the first are set in `text-2` so the word still scans as one. Term and meaning wrap, because they are the content, but a row shows at most three lines of term and two of meaning so one long card cannot fill the screen; the open card shows the rest. Below 36 rem of list width, a container query drops the meaning under the term, so the phone and any narrow list share one rule. The AI's field-source mark stays in the open card: when the AI writes most meanings, a mark on every row says nothing. A card the learner marked important carries a small filled star after its term in `amber-mark`, because it is the learner's own choice and a few of them are worth finding in a list; a screen reader hears Important with the state.

Filter and Sort sit above the list, with search beside them on desktop where `/` lands; on the phone search sits in the top bar and replaces it while open. Filter is one menu that stays open while choosing: checkbox rows for state, for section when the deck has sections, and for tag when its cards carry tags, each tag with its card count, most used first; and a radio set for when a card is due, Any time, Due today or Due this week. Ticking two tags shows cards carrying either. Each active filter shows as a chip that removes it, a tag's reading "Tag: food", with Clear filters once there are two. Sort is a menu: Section, offered only in a deck with sections and the default there, groups the words under each section in the deck's order, oldest card first, with the cards without a section last; When it's back groups them under Due now, This week, Later and Not started, and is the only sort that shows a date in the row; Recently added groups them by the day they arrived and is the default in a deck without sections; A–Z is one list. Filters and sort belong to the visit and reset when the learner leaves. On the phone, Section and Tags open a second level of the Filter sheet, and the tab bar reviews what a filter shows that is due ([phone.md](system/phone.md#navigation)).

A deck with sections reads as a list of lessons. Each section is a heading with its count. While the deck opens sections one after another, Today's plate gains a second row under a hairline that names the section the learner is on and the one next step, so a long deck never needs scrolling to find where to go on: what opens the next section, in cards rather than a percentage, over a thin Known track with a tick at the threshold; **Start** once the next section is ready, which takes the amber only when nothing is due, since the page never has two amber buttons, and appears only in a deck the owner set to start sections by hand, since by default the next section opens by itself on the grade that makes it ready; and **Go to**, which clears filters and scrolls the list to that section's heading. The heading of the current section carries **You are here**, a green chip with a pin, `canvas` on `good`, so the scroll lands somewhere the eye can find; green rather than amber, because it says where the learner is and asks for nothing. A section that is not open still lists its cards, because the learner should be able to read what is coming: its heading and rows are quieter, a lock takes the state's icon on each card that has not come up yet, and a card opens as usual. A ready section has a `plate-2` well above its heading with Start; a locked one offers **Start anyway** in its heading: as the first item of the section's menu when the heading has one, so the title keeps its line and the menu stays beside it, and as a quiet button otherwise; when that would also open locked sections before it, a dialog says which and how many cards join the reviews, because a start is never undone. Nothing folds.

The owner moves cards from the list and arranges sections in deck settings. A section's heading has a menu with Add card here, Rename and Arrange sections; the deck's menu adds New section, and every learner's menu has Select cards. Every learner's menu on a section with cards due today starts with **Review this section**, a review of that section alone, so a lesson can be refreshed ahead of a backlog; the count is the section's own share of the deck's due count, so a section not open yet offers it once a card there has started. It runs to the section's end like a deck review, and a member's menu holds only that. A card drags onto any section, with a long press on touch; the lifted copy is the row itself on an `edge-2` plate at 1.02×, and it goes the moment it drops, because the card is already in its new place. Select cards swaps each row's state icon for a checkbox of the same 15 px, so choosing moves nothing in the list; shift-click selects a range. While choosing, the row that holds Filter and Sort becomes the selection toolbar and sticks to the top of the page: ✕ to stop, the count, Select all or Clear, and one amber **Actions** menu, so the bar stays calm however many things can be done. Its first group reviews: **Review 2 due** takes the chosen cards due today, and **Review all 5** every chosen card that has started and was not reviewed today, due ones first and the rest in the order they would fall due; each says how many, since new cards start in the day's draw and a card reviewed today has had its turn. Under a separator, the owner's **Tags…** and **Move to section…**, which opens the same picker a card's own menu does. A member's menu holds only the review group, because changing the cards stays the owner's. Filtering and then Select all is how a learner reviews a tag or a search, such as every food word the night before a class. The review is a round: it ends on Round done, and Continue goes on to the day's draw. One review takes at most 200 cards. Tags… opens a dialog listing the tags the chosen cards carry, then the learner's other tags, each with a checkbox and "2 of 3": a tag on every card is ticked, one on some is mixed, and a press puts it on every card or takes it off every card. A search field above the list finds a tag in any case and offers Create for a new name, which arrives ticked. Save writes only the cards that change, a card already holding twenty tags is left without the new one and the toast says so, and Undo puts every card's tags back. It covers nothing, so the phone's tab bar stays, and Escape leaves unless a menu or dialog is open. A section with no cards shows a dashed well to drop into. Order is what every learner opens sections in, so it changes only in the Sections group of deck settings, never by a stray drag: with arrows on the web, beside rename, archive and New section, and on the phone by a handle that Edit shows, on a page that draws the sections as the path a learner walks; Archived sections appears once there is one to restore. Under the list, **How sections open** is three cards: one after another automatically, the default; one after another when the learner starts them; or all at once. Moves apply at once and undo with a toast. Archiving a section with cards is the one move that asks first on the web, because the owner chooses whether its cards leave with it; the phone archives at once, keeping its cards in the deck, with Undo. On the phone a section at rest swipes to Rename and Archive, Edit appears once there are two sections to order, and Select cards is in the deck's menu for any deck with cards in it, a member's included. The selection toolbar floats as a glass bar above the tab bar, as Photos and Mail pin theirs: ✕, the count, Select all, and an amber ⋯ that opens iOS's own menu of what can be done to the chosen cards. Its first group is the same **Review 2 due** and **Review all 5** as the web's, each hidden when it would take no cards, and the review ends on Round done. Next, **Mark important** marks every chosen card, or **Unmark important** once every chosen card is marked; the mark is the learner's own, so a member has it too. Under a separator, the owner's Move to section when the deck has sections, Move to deck when the learner has another deck, and Archive. Each change acts at once and offers Undo, which gives every card back what it had; a later action, such as tags, is one more row. Filter and Sort dim while choosing, so what is chosen never changes under a filter. Go to, the lock on a card that has not come up, and the owner's Add card here work as on the web; on the phone Add card here opens capture with that section chosen.

A word opens where it can be read with the list. When the page is wide enough for a 640 px list and the 400 px card, which is a 1280 px window beside the rail, the card sits beside the list on a plate, in its own sticky scroll. When it is not, the card is a side sheet on the same plate at the end edge over a dimmed page on a desktop, and a drawer over the whole screen on touch. The shape follows the window, so a resize moves an open card between beside and sheet; it waits while a field is being edited or the Move sheet is open, so the switch never drops what is in progress. J and K walk the list in its sorted order, Escape closes the card, except while a field is being edited, when it only cancels the edit, and the open card lives in `?card=`, so Back closes it.

Deck actions live behind one menu: Deck settings opens a screen, and the name is changed there rather than inline, Export writes a CSV, Archive leaves the deck list with an Undo toast. Cards archive the same way, from the row.

The **AI** group in deck settings is the owner's alone: one card holding a checkbox per field the AI may fill (Meaning, Example, Notes, Pronunciation) and an optional **Instructions** box. A checkbox saves when pressed and the instructions save when the field is left, like the rest of the screen. The fields start from the default for the deck's language. A member's About this deck shows neither.

A member of a shared deck is offered none of those, because none of them would work: no Add card, no Export, no Archive, and a card carries no overflow menu at all once Enrich, Move to…, Move to section… and Archive are gone with it. The menu holds About this deck and Leave deck, and the deck's subline names the owner the way the Library card does, so the screen says whose deck it is before it says what cannot be done to it. About this deck stands where the owner's settings do: the owner with one line on what they control, the deck's description, language, direction and card count as facts rather than disabled fields, and Leave deck under them. Leaving is the one archive-shaped move that asks first rather than undoing from a toast, because the way back is the join link or the deck's public page and the owner may have closed both; the dialog says the reviews are kept, and the safe button is Keep studying.

A deck is a name and two settings, so making one is a sheet and not a wizard. The name is the one field that matters and takes the large input; language and direction already have an answer, so they sit under it as a select and three segments. Creating it lands the learner in the empty deck, which is where the words go next. Everything chosen there is changeable afterwards.

Changing it is a screen, `/library/$deckId/settings`, because on the phone it should push in and the back gesture should work, and because the direction choice needs room to say what it does: three rows, each spelling out what will be shown, using a real card from the deck. Selection is carried by the edge and a filled dot, both ink. Nothing on the screen has a Save button. A choice is made when it is made, a name commits on blur and on the way out, and one quiet "Saved" in the header says so. What changing the direction does to the cards already in the deck is [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-a-decks-direction-is-a-filter-not-a-migration.md).

The owner's deck settings carry a **Tags** group: one row naming how many tags the deck's cards carry, with Manage, that opens the deck's Tags page, `/library/$deckId/settings/tags`, with Settings as its back link. The page has a search field, then In use, each tag with its card count and a ⋯ menu of Rename…, Merge into… and Archive, then Archived tags, each with Restore. Every change stays in this deck, because a tag is the learner's across decks and a deck's settings should not reach into others: Rename retags this deck's cards, and renaming onto a name the learner already uses merges the two, which the field says before it happens and the button reads Merge; Merge into… picks one of the deck's other tags. A change of case alone respells the tag everywhere, since one name is one tag. Archive takes the tag off this deck's cards and offers Undo; Restore puts it back on the same cards in the same place, except a card that has filled its twenty since. A rename offers Undo, and a merge does not, because the merged cards can no longer be told apart. A member sees no Tags group: the owner's tags are theirs to filter by, not to change.

A review card's top line names where the card came from: the deck with its book icon, then the mode, and the state chip at the end. A review can draw from every deck, so the deck is always there; the card's language code follows the mode only when it differs from the deck's.

A card's picture is content, not decoration, so it follows the word's rules. In a picture mode it is the cue, on the start edge like the word, up to 30% of the viewport or 240 px tall, and it keeps that size on reveal while the target and the other text field rise in under the rule. In a text mode the picture joins the context under the target at up to 120 px. The card page shows it at up to 140 px on its own plate, with the description wrapped to the picture's width under it, so the two read as one thing. `CardPicture` holds the picture's own shape from the first frame over a `plate-2` well, fades it in with `enter-fade`, and then drops the well, because a road sign on transparency would otherwise sit in a grey box. When it cannot load it says "Couldn't load the picture" and shows the description in its place, so the card can still be revealed and graded. The card page says in words when picture review is waiting for a picture or a description.

Pictures are kept for offline review in a Cache Storage bucket per learner, and every other learner's bucket is dropped, so a shared browser never shows one learner's pictures to another. The picture's URL carries its id, so a replaced picture is a new entry, and the server sends `private, no-store`, so the HTTP cache holds nothing. When Cache Storage refuses, the picture still loads, it just is not kept offline.

Library lists the decks without a series first, as one plate of rows with no heading, because they are simply the learner's decks. Each series follows under its own heading: its name and a menu with New deck, Edit series, Move up, Move down and Delete series, where New deck makes the deck inside that series. On the phone the menu has Choose decks, Rename series, Reorder series once there are two, and Delete series; Reorder series opens a sheet of every series that drags by its handles and saves as each drops. A long name truncates rather than pushing the menu under it. The series' decks follow in one plate in the series' order, and while any is due **Review all** sits under the plate: a strip in the due count's `amber-soft`, as wide as the plate and tucked beneath its bottom edge so the plate rests on it, with the series' due count and a chevron in the same columns as its rows' counts and chevrons, that reviews every deck in the series. It is a tint, never the primary amber, so Today's Review stays the one primary, and it steps aside when nothing is due. While a deck is dragged over a series, its plate fills with `plate-2`. The learner's own decks can be dragged into, out of and along a series; a long press starts the drag on touch, Space on the keyboard, and Enter still opens the deck. A deck without a series keeps its place by creation date, so the loose list takes a drop without making room, and when every deck is in a series a dashed target appears below them only while a deck is dragged, as the way out of one. The arrow keys can carry a deck out of its series as well as along it. The same moves exist without dragging: **New series** in Library's menu, **Edit series** (a name and a checklist of decks with up and down arrows), and **Move to series** in a deck's menu. Deleting a series always asks, because it is the one write with no Undo: the series is gone for good, and when it holds decks the learner also chooses whether they are archived with it or stay loose in Library. Everything else about a series undoes with a toast.

A deck in Library is a row in its group's plate, drawn like Today's deck rows, because Library is for choosing a deck to open: the whole row is the link and ends in Go. The first line is the name, which inside a series drops the series' own name and the separator after it, so "Eesti A1 · Words" reads "Words"; the second is the deck's language when it has one, the card count, and, with nothing due, when the next card is back. The due count sits just before Go's chevron while cards are due, so the two read as one end of the row. A joined deck adds its owner on a third line: a deck added from Explore draws [the publisher's mark](explore.md#the-publishers-mark) there, and a deck joined through a private link keeps the learner plate, because only publishing makes a photo public. The split between states is the deck's progress, not something to choose by, so it lives on the deck page and the row carries no stripe: due is its only colour, and a page of decks with nothing due stays quiet. A row of new cards names its actor, because a card an integration wrote must never look like one the learner typed.

**Search** and **New deck** sit beside Library's menu on a desktop, as icon buttons. Search opens into a field there with an X inside that closes it, and an empty field closes on its own when focus leaves it. On the phone, where the title has no room beside it, the field and a New deck button share a row under the title. Search narrows Library as the learner types, by a deck's name, its language or its series' name: a series none of whose decks match steps aside, nothing can be dragged until the search is cleared, and with nothing left Library says so and offers Clear search. `/` reaches it from anywhere on the screen. Library ends with its last group: Archived decks sits in Library's menu while any deck is archived, because deleting a series can archive its decks, and Archived is otherwise reached from You.

## Adding and editing a card

A card is added and changed with one form, `CardForm`, so everything the API can set on a card can be set in the app, in one place. Term, meaning and deck come first, because capture during a lesson needs nothing else. The deck is the one the sheet was opened from, or the deck last added to.

Everything else a card holds waits until it is asked for, in one row of chips under the deck, the same on a desktop and on touch, as on the phone ([phone.md](system/phone.md)). Each chip is its field's icon and its label, with no other mark, and the row is one line that scrolls sideways, fading at each edge that has more; a mouse wheel scrolls it too. A chip for text, such as notes, example, pronunciation, source or the picture, adds its field in place under the fields already shown, with the caret in it, and a field once shown stays until the next card, even emptied. A chip for a choice opens its list, a panel on a desktop and a drawer on touch, and then shows what was chosen: the section, only when the deck has sections, reads its name; tags opens a search of the learner's tags in use with Create for a new one, and reads the one tag or how many; how it's asked reads its label while it follows the deck, the mode for one, or how many, and lists the picture modes only once the card has a picture. A chip that counts several names lists them on hover. The section leads and how it's asked ends the row; between them the chips run from the field the deck's cards hold most, as the deck page last read them, and a deck with no cards yet starts from the fields its AI may fill. Adding offers no hook and no language: a hook is for a card that keeps failing, and the language follows the deck. Editing shows every field the card holds and offers the hook and the language as chips too. A field refused on submit is already on the form, with the message under it.

Adding closes the sheet and says "Added" in a toast. "Create more", a checkbox beside the action and remembered per learner, keeps the sheet open instead: "Added" or "is already in" then appears under the term, and language, source and tags stay for the next card. ⌘ Enter adds from any field. A drawer has no Cancel, because a swipe or a tap outside puts it away. Closing a sheet that holds anything typed says "Card discarded" with Undo, which brings the draft back, picture included; closing an edit with unsaved changes says "Changes discarded" the same way. A picture is sent after the card exists, since the picture routes need its id; if it fails the card is still added, the message says so, and its Add picture opens that card's edit sheet at the picture field.

The card page reads as a page. Marking a card important is the first item of its menu, Mark important or Unmark important, offered to the owner and a member alike because the mark is their own; a member's menu holds only that. The bar gets no button for it, because the bar is for reading and moving between cards. A marked card's meta line under the term starts with the amber star and the word Important, so the state is never the colour alone. Review's card menu offers the same item to anyone. Nothing on it is edited in place, because a page where any text can turn into a field is a page where a tap to read becomes an edit. The pencil in its bar opens the same form, filled in, and Save writes only what changed. A description that names the term or the meaning is refused before anything is sent, by the same rule the server applies.

A field the AI wrote carries the AI badge beside its label, and the pronunciation carries one under the term, where there is no label to sit on. Nothing else on the page is marked: the learner's own words and the lesson's are the ordinary case, and a page where every field is labelled labels nothing. The badge belongs to the label rather than the far edge of the panel, so it reads as part of the field's name. Editing a field makes it the learner's and the badge goes with the same write. The text itself is ordinary text in either case, because the page is for reading the meaning.

While an add's enrichment is outstanding, each field it may fill holds its space with a shimmer: two lines under Meaning, Example and Notes, one under the term for the pronunciation, and a short one in the meta line for a language the card has not got. A field the AI chose to skip stops shimmering and stays empty. The reads that already load the deck's cards carry the card's `enrichmentStatus`, so the page asks again every two seconds while a card is working and the text lands without a reload. A card that ends at `failed` stops shimmering and shows what it has. There is no spinner and no badge for the waiting itself, and a list row shows nothing new: a row that says "No meaning yet" is already saying it.

Enrich in the card's overflow menu asks for the same fill by hand, under the same deck rules, and shows while the language or a field the deck lets the AI fill is still empty: a card the AI left alone, one whose job failed, and one added before enrichment existed all reach it the same way. Only the card's owner is offered it, so a member of a shared deck sees no such item. A card at `failed` carries one quiet line under its fields, "Couldn’t enrich this card." with Try again, which is the only place the failure is named: nothing about it reaches a list row or the capture sheet. The card moves to `working` as the request returns, so the shimmer starts without a second state, and asking on a card with nothing left to fill is refused rather than accepted in silence.

## Notes

A card's notes are Markdown source in a small subset, so a breakdown such as *hea aeg* → *head aega* reads clearly in review and on the card page. The subset is paragraphs, line breaks, `**bold**` or `__bold__`, `*italic*` or `_italic_`, `++underline++`, `~~strikethrough~~`, links (`[text](https://example.com)` or `<https://example.com>`, with `http`, `https` or `mailto` destinations), bulleted lists (`-`, `+` or `*`) and numbered lists (`1.` or `1)`), with a backslash to keep a marker literal. A single line break stays a line break, so a plain note written before formatting reads as it did. Everything else, including headings, images, tables, code and raw HTML, shows as the text that was typed, and nothing in a note is ever rendered as HTML. `parseNotes` in `packages/core/src/notes.ts` is the one reader, so the app, and later the public catalog, render the same thing.

Review's answer side and the card page render the formatting in the size and colour of the notes around it, with list markers in `muted`. Links have an underline, a visible keyboard focus ring and open in a new tab so following one keeps the current review; relative destinations and other schemes stay literal text. Wherever notes are quoted inside a sentence, such as the Activity line "Notes changed to …", or matched by search, only the words count, through `notesToText`. The API, MCP and CSV export carry the source unchanged, and the 2,000-character limit counts the source. The card form's notes stay a plain textarea with no hint, toolbar or preview: the syntax is ordinary Markdown, learned by using it. Term, meaning, example, deck descriptions and imported notes stay plain text.

# The phone app

The phone app in `apps/mobile` takes the tokens, the lantern, the flame, the reveal, the end of a review and the moments in [motion.md](motion.md) from `packages/design`, and keeps the web's component names, Onest and Lucide. This file lists only where the phone differs, each time because touch or iOS gives it a better way. Lymi draws its persistent chrome and every row and icon; Apple draws the sheets they sit in, the share sheet, alerts, the keyboard and permission prompts ([ADR 0029](../../adr/0029-the-phone-app-is-expo-react-native-sharing-core.md)). Apple also draws the command menus that grow out of a button, with Lymi's Lucide icons made into custom symbols ([Menus](#menus)). Which jobs the phone leads is in [PRODUCT.md](../../../PRODUCT.md#surfaces). The iOS app is for iPhone only: an iPad runs it in its iPhone-sized window, and an iPad learner who wants the wide layout uses the web, which has the rail and the column from 768 px.

## Navigation

- The tabs are iOS's own tab bar, in Onest with Lucide icons: Today, Library, Explore and You, each an icon over its label. The chosen tab is amber on the system's lens, its icon filled in `amber-mark` and its label in `amber-text`, so the choice reads by shape as well as colour ([colour.md](colour.md#amber-means-act)); the rest stay outlined in iOS's monochrome style. `pnpm --filter @lymi/mobile tab-icons` draws both from Lucide. Tapping the current tab returns its stack to the top. Before iOS 26 it is the system's classic bar ([ADR 0034](../../adr/0034-the-phone-uses-the-system-tab-bar-and-captures-from-the-top-bar.md)).
- Every screen's top bar is pinned, with its actions as glass buttons at its end. A tab's large title starts in the bar and shrinks to an inline title at its start as the page scrolls; a page's title travels from under the bar into its middle, and its named back folds into a round glass back. A page title too long for one line, such as a long deck name, wraps in the page instead, and the bar's title fades in as it goes under the bar. Library, a deck, Sections and Explore refresh with iOS's pull to refresh (`Screen`'s `onRefresh`). What scrolls beneath softens into the room, never at rest, so nothing crosses the lantern's light.
- **You** gathers what is about the learner: their photo, name and email at the top, where a tap opens a sheet of Lymi's rows, as a Select does, to rename and to take, choose or remove the photo; Insights as one plate that opens the full page, carrying the month's recall and the thirty-day strip once there is a review; then the reminder time, then the language and the theme in one group, then Activity, feedback and sign-out. The phone has no Settings page: You holds every setting the web's Settings does. The language belongs to the account, so a change holds on the web and meanings follow it; the theme and the reminder time belong to the phone, as the web keeps them per browser. Between Activity and feedback, **Your data** holds Import, Export library, API keys and Connected apps, each on the phone ([ADR 0036](../../adr/0036-setup-runs-on-the-phone.md)): Export library opens its sheet and the rest push pages. API keys and Connected apps keep the web's rows, a key is made in a sheet whose ✓ creates it and is then shown once with Copy key, revoking a key or disconnecting an app is in its row's ⋯ and asks in iOS's alert, since neither can be undone, and each page ends with a Docs group whose rows say what each page of the docs holds. Publishing and deck reports, done with an API key, show only to an account with a published deck and open the API reference. Delete account closes You, after sign-out, in danger: it pushes a page that counts what goes, names the decks that stay for others and opens Export library, then confirms with an emailed code and signs out. Archived closes Library.
- Capture is an amber glass button at the end of every tab's top bar, beside the streak. A tap opens Add a word as a sheet from below; a long press grows iOS's menu out of it with Add a card, Smart add and New deck, so the tap stays one word's quickest way in. Explore has neither: its bar holds Filter and Search in one glass capsule, because finding a deck there is the job, and holds nothing while no deck is published, and a word is caught from any other tab.
- Library's bar holds what Library is for instead: search, then the amber plus, iOS's own menu growing out of it (Add a card, Smart add, New deck, New series, Import decks, which opens Import). The streak stays on Today.
- Search sits at the foot of the screen and rides on the keyboard, as iOS 26's own search does: a glass field and a round glass close, with a filter between them where the screen has one. The screen above it is empty until the first letters are typed. Every search on the phone uses `SearchBar`.
- A deck opens on one summary plate, the count to review beside the deck's size, its cards' states as one bar with their counts under it, then a full-width Review. Once Review scrolls under the bar, the tab bar's accessory takes over, showing how many and a play button as Podcasts shows what is playing; scrolled back, it goes. Before iOS 26 there is no accessory, so the deck's own Review is the only one.
- Under the plate sit Filter and Sort. With filters on, Filter is ink, as a chosen chip is, with how many are on in a `canvas` badge, and the filters themselves are read and removed in its sheet. Filter is one sheet: state and when cards are due are chosen on its first level, and Section and Tags, each shown only when the deck has some, are rows that name what is picked and open their list on a level of their own in the same sheet, with a back chevron at the title. While the list is filtered, the summary plate, its Review and the accessory still mean the whole deck; the filtered cards are reviewed by Select cards, Select all, then Review due or Review all in its Actions menu, as on the web.

## On Android

Android keeps the same screens, tabs, words and rooms, and takes Material's conventions where its system owns the gesture or the chrome. Every difference lives in `src/ui` and `src/theme`, as a `.android.tsx` twin or a constant there; screens and data never branch on the platform.

- The tabs are Material's navigation bar in the web rail's colours: `rail` behind, every tab labelled, and the chosen tab on a `rail-chosen` pill with its icon filled and amber, as on iOS, never the wallpaper's colours.
- A page's back is a bare arrow, and its title docks at the start beside it as the page scrolls. A tab's bar keeps the streak and the amber capture.
- Back does what the screen's own close does: in a review it ends on Review done, and Welcome steps back through its questions.
- Sheets are Material's bottom sheets on the room's `sheet` colour, holding Lymi's groups and rows under Lymi's own grabber rather than Material's taller handle; a form sheet fills the window and gets the same grabber. Commands from a button drop down as Lymi's own menu, drawn as the web's dropdown with the same icons as iOS's and, from Android 12, frosted over the screens as the web's glass is (`ui/blur-root.tsx`), and a long press opens the row's commands as a sheet. Every stack, a tab's too, takes the same sheet setup from `ui/stack.tsx`.
- Android takes both screen edges for Back, so a swipe that mirrors iOS's edge swipe, such as taking back a grade, starts anywhere on the card (`ui/edge-swipe.ts`).
- The status bar's icons follow the room, not the phone's setting. The keyboard, the clock dialog and permission prompts are the system's.

## How a screen arrives

| Motion | For | Leaves by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Push from the side | A page to read or work in: a deck, a card, a series, Insights, Activity, a deck in Explore | The named back, or a swipe from the start edge |
| Sheet from below | A task with an end: Add a word, New deck, Edit card, a choice list such as the daily goal, the card menu in a review, the streak | A swipe down, with detents where the task grows |
| Cover from below | A mode that takes the screen: a review | X, or pulling the top bar down |
| Fade in place | A mode that replaces the screen: Welcome and sign-in | Finishing, or Skip for now |

This is [overlays.md](overlays.md)'s rule in iOS's own navigation: from the side means back, from below means swipe down.

The streak is the one place that differs from the web, where touch shows it as a page from the end edge ([streak.md](../streak.md)). On the phone it peeks at the run and today and drags up for the month, so glancing at it never leaves the screen under it. Its daily goal is a choice list in a sheet of its own over it: the streak first rises to full height so the goal's sheet stacks visibly on top, and peeks again once it closes. A preset is saved once the sheet has gone, a number of one's own steps on its row's − and +, and with no connection the list says it needs one.

## Components

The phone's components are in `apps/mobile/src/ui` under the web's names, and follow [forms.md](forms.md), [buttons.md](buttons.md) and [chips-and-marks.md](chips-and-marks.md). They differ in these ways:

- Every control is 44 pt, so a `md` button is 44 pt tall and `sm` reaches 44 pt through its hit area.
- `xl` is Today's Review button alone, 64 pt with a 20 pt label as on the web. Buttons that stack at the foot, such as the end of a review and Welcome's Continue, are `lg`, 48 pt, since three at 64 pt outweighed the count above them.
- `Sheet` is iOS's own sheet with Lymi's content, so it is Liquid Glass, follows the finger and swipes away. It opens at the height of what it holds. Its title and description sit centred in its header ([Sheets](#sheets)); every `SheetHeading`, `SheetNote` and `SheetError` starts on one line, 16 pt in from a plate's edge where a row's icon or check starts, as iOS sets a grouped list's headers, so a screen never sets its own inset. Rows that belong together share one plate inset 16 pt from the sheet's edge, 12 pt from the next, split by full-width hairlines, as every plate in Lymi is. A row is 52 pt. A group whose rows carry an icon or a check keeps a slot for it on every row, so labels line up; a group with neither has no slot. A removal is red, a chosen row has a check, a row that opens more ends in a chevron in `muted`, and a figure such as a count sits at the end as its `value`. A sheet with more to show peeks at its first group and drags up to the full screen, where iOS makes it opaque and the sheet colour keeps the plates apart, as the dev menu does. `Select` is a sheet of rows, each with an optional second line; a choice runs once the sheet has gone. A `Select` past fifteen fixed options, such as Language, is the phone's Combobox: a search field leads and takes focus, the list under it keeps one height and scrolls on its own while it filters, the first match is lit as a pressed row is and Return takes it, a drag hides the keyboard, and a row takes the first tap with the keyboard up. Which kind of sheet a task gets is in [Sheets](#sheets).
- A sheet's own controls float on it as glass: a round 44 pt button in each top corner, close at the start and the sheet's action at the end, tinted amber, with a glass pill between them for what the sheet works in, such as the deck.
- Chips come in `sm` and `md`, and a state chip is one size wherever it shows, Forgotten included; the AI badge is the `sm` chip. A due count has one size. The web's `xs` chip, `lg` chip, `lg` due count and `xs` icon button have no phone use yet.
- `Switch` is drawn by Lymi, amber when on, so it sits in a form beside the other controls.
- Every error line is `FieldError` from `ui/field.tsx`: the alert icon centres on the text's first line however it wraps, and VoiceOver reads it as it appears. It starts 4 pt in, where `FieldDescription` and a form's other lines outside a plate start, or flush (`flush`) under a field drawn as a line with no box, as in Add a word. Biome refuses a hand-made one.
- Every spring comes from `@lymi/design`: `withSpring(value, spring(PRESET))`, or `springIn(new Builder(), PRESET)` for a layout animation. A single physics value such as `.damping(18)` keeps Reanimated 4's heavy defaults beside it and overshoots, so Biome refuses it.
- Tabular figures are for a figure on its own that changes in place or lines up in a column, such as a due count. A number inside a phrase, such as "21 cards", keeps Onest's own figures, or a 1 sits apart from its neighbours.
- React Native ignores `text-end` and `text-start`, so end-aligned text takes `TEXT_END` from `theme/type.ts`, and Biome refuses the classes.
- A card's own words, its term, reading and example, pass the card's language to `Text` as `lang`. React Native's text carries no language, so on a non-Japanese iPhone the system's fallback from Onest gives kanji that Chinese shares their Chinese forms; for a card whose language names a script face (`languageScript`), `Text` draws that script's runs in the first face of its `--face-<code>` token, Hiragino Sans for Japanese and PingFang SC or TC for simplified or traditional Chinese, and leaves the rest in Onest, as the web's `:lang()` rules do. A reading written in letters, as pinyin and Jyutping are, keeps a word space's room in all, an eighth of its size each side (`readingInset`), so a syllable wider than its characters never runs into the next. Android's fallback has no family to name, so it keeps the device's choice.
- A card's term is `TermText` and its example `ExampleText`, both from `ui/term-text.tsx`: they choose between drawing a reading and plain text, so no screen asks for a term's reading itself. Furigana is `Furigana` from `ui/furigana.tsx`. React Native has no ruby, so each run is a column with its reading over it, and the columns wrap: a run with a reading never breaks, plain kana and kanji break between characters, and a closing mark or small kana stays on the line before. The reading is half the text's drawn size and never under 10 pt, in `text-2` at 400; a reading in letters is 0.4 of it and never over the 15.5 pt lede, scaled with Dynamic Type, since it runs several letters to a character, and a Jyutping tone number is raised at 0.7 of its size, so every line of a Japanese term stands a reading's height taller whether or not it has one. A hidden reading keeps its room and fades in at the reveal. VoiceOver hears the reading in place of the kanji while it shows, and the term as written while it is hidden. A list of Japanese terms keeps the room on rows without one (`room`), as the web's deck does. A term's forms flow after it as `tail` and wrap with it; a row cut at its lines ends in an ellipsis and keeps the ★ (`after`) past the cut. Text with no reading to draw stays one plain `Text`, and only a review cue, which passes `hidden`, sets up the fade. A Russian term whose pronunciation marks its stress is drawn as that marked form, за́мок, in the same plain `Text`; `hidden` draws the plain term instead, with no fade. `ExampleText` given the learner's Known pairs (`known`, from `useKnownReadings`) keeps the room of a reading over a word they know but draws nothing there, and VoiceOver hears that word as written.
- An order that is dragged uses `DragList` from `ui/drag-list.tsx`: a grip at each row's end lifts it, the rest make room, and it saves as it drops. Every row also offers Move up and Move down to VoiceOver. Sections in Edit and Series order use it.
- A bar that floats above the tab bar, such as the selection toolbar, goes through `useFloatingBar`: the root draws it, since only the root knows where the screen ends, it leaves when its screen loses focus, and a toast rises above it. It slides in rather than fading, because glass that starts transparent never draws its effect.
- A list that can run to hundreds of rows, such as a deck's words, goes through `Screen`'s `list`, which mounts only the rows near the screen; each row is memoized, so a change elsewhere on the page redraws only rows whose card changed. Every row with a long-press menu carries several native views, so a deck of 3,000 mounted whole took 14 s to open.
- A stepper's − and + repeat while held (`IconButton`'s `repeat`) and dim at the bounds.
- A page's intro sentence is `Screen`'s `description`, held 8 pt under the large title. A page whose header holds its name passes `titleAt`, so the bar's title arrives as the name goes under the bar, and a page with its own `ground` colours its bar with it.

The design screen shows each component in every state. Open `lymi://design`, as the web has `/design`; each canvas has its own switch for the other room.

## Empty states

`EmptyState` in `ui/empty-state.tsx` is every place with nothing to show, following [empty-states.md](empty-states.md)'s rules in one component rather than the web's several. Why the place is empty picks the icon, and where it sits picks the size.

- **Kinds:** `start` for nothing made yet, `noMatch` for a search or filter that matched nothing, `failed` for a read that failed, and `offline` for a place that needs a connection the phone does not have. Only `failed` is red; being offline is no failure.
- **Sizes:** `page` when the whole screen is empty, large and centred in the room the bars leave, with a primary button; `inline` inside a plate already on the screen, which is its surface, with a small secondary button. It never brings a plate of its own.
- The icon sits in a soft disc, never the lantern, and the title names what is missing: "No decks match “map”", not "Nothing here". Still loading is a skeleton, never an empty state.

## Sheets

Every sheet rises from below and swipes away. What the task holds decides its kind, and the kind decides its glass and its fills:

| Kind | For | Built with | Surface | Fills |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Row sheet | A choice or a one-field edit over the screen: a `Select`, Filter (which saves), a deck's name, a section's name, the learner's name and photo, the hook | `Sheet`, inside the screen | iOS's glass, untinted, at the height of what it holds | On-glass |
| Peek sheet | A screen to glance at that drags up for more: the streak, the cards an Activity row wrote | `Sheet` with `more`, or a route presented as `formSheet` with detents | Glass while it peeks; iOS makes it opaque at full height | On-glass |
| Short form | A form that fits on one screen and is done in seconds: Add a word, Edit card, New deck, New series, Share deck | A route presented as `formSheet`. Add a word, Edit card and New deck open at full height, so iOS draws them opaque; New series sizes to its fields and Share deck peeks | Glass below full height; opaque at full height | On-glass, reading on either |
| Long form | A form that scrolls or holds long text: feedback, Choose decks, Smart add | A route presented as `formSheet` at full height | Opaque, the sheet colour | The room's own, since on-glass fills vanish against an opaque sheet |

- Glass wherever the task is quick; opaque where the learner reads or types at length. A form that grows several sections or a long text field becomes a long form. iOS draws any sheet opaque at full height, so a short form stays glass only while it opens below full height.
- With glass off, under Reduce Transparency or before iOS 26, every glass sheet is the opaque sheet colour and its groups take the room's fills, so no kind turns into white on white. Each kind is checked in that state before it ships.
- Reach for a row sheet first. A one-field edit belongs in one: the field is a `bg-glass-plate` box with no label over it, since the sheet's title names it, 44 pt for one line and growing with its text; a multi-line field takes `MULTILINE` from `theme/type.ts`, since iOS pins its text to the top. A text that runs to a paragraph, such as a deck's instructions for the AI, gets a tall box from the start.
- Every sheet with a title has one header, which `Sheet` draws and no screen draws for itself: its title centred at `text-md`, with its description under it. A sheet that holds something to save, a form or a list ticked several at a time, adds close at the start and an amber ✓ at the end, which `Sheet`'s `save` draws; ✓ and the keyboard's done key save, and close and a swipe leave without saving. A sheet that saves nothing, such as a list where a tap is the choice, shows its title alone, with no buttons, and a swipe or the tap closes it. A sheet without a title, a row's commands or a peek led by its own content, has no header. Nothing saves with a full-width button under a field. `/design`'s Sheet header shows each case.
- A sheet's title may carry a short line centred under it, `Sheet`'s `description`, two lines at most in every language, when they say what the rows can't: what the choice changes, such as a card leaving its section, a rule behind it, such as every grade counting toward the goal, or why the sheet can't act while offline. How to use the rows never goes there, and a sheet whose rows speak for themselves has none. A `SheetNote` under a group is for that group alone. The header keeps 20 pt of room before the first group.
- A number that steps, such as a daily goal of one's own, is a stepper row rather than a number pad, so its sheet keeps its height and its glass and no second Done appears over the keys.
- A form keeps its fields as labelled lines with no box ([Add a word](#add-a-word-and-edit-card)); only a card's term is set large. Its one action is amber at the end of the header, and close or a swipe leaves without saving. Once something is typed, a swipe asks before it throws the draft away.
- One of a list runs once the sheet has gone, everywhere. Several of a list are ticked as a draft that ✓ saves, as Filter, a card's tags, how it's asked and the fields AI fills do; close and a swipe leave them as they were.
- Filters sit in one sheet that saves: close at the start, Filter centred, an amber ✓ that applies at the end, and centred under the title, as `Sheet`'s `subtitle`, how many cards the drafted filters keep. Every filter is a `SheetHeading` over one plate of rows whose labels share a line: State's checkboxes, Due's Any time, Today and This week as a radio, each with the cards it keeps, then Section and Tags as one row each, its icon in the slot and its label naming what is picked or Any section and Any tag, that opens its list on a second level of the same sheet. There a back chevron takes close's place, the list keeps the first level's height and scrolls within it, since iOS snaps a sheet to a new height rather than gliding, and it fades in from the side the learner is going toward; past fifteen rows a search field leads, as a long `Select`'s does. Under the last group, Reset filters is a centred text action with its icon, set apart from the sections because it acts on the whole sheet, shown once anything is drafted. Close and a swipe leave the filters as they were. Filter is the one sheet with a second level.
- A row sheet never opens a sheet, the camera or the photo library over itself, and never moves to another screen while it is open. A row that needs one closes the sheet and acts once it has gone. Add a word's picture sheet is the exception: iOS's camera and photo picker open over it, so the picture comes back to the sheet where it is described. A form may open a row sheet over itself, which stacks on it; a part-height sheet cannot, because iOS pushes it out of view under the new one, so a part-height sheet first rises to full height and the new sheet stacks over it, as the streak does for its daily goal.
- A `formSheet` route lays out at most a header and one scroll view. A header beside the scroll view takes `collapsable={false}`, or iOS lays the scroll view over it.
- Every row sheet has a title in its header. A peek sheet and a form carry their controls as glass in the top corners, as a sheet that saves does.
- A form's header is one shared row: close at the start, named Cancel when leaving discards something and Close when it does not; its name centred at `text-md`, or a pill for what it works in, such as Add a word's deck; the one action at the end. Nothing under it repeats the name as a large title; a line saying what the sheet is for comes first in the content.
- A field or search box on a sheet iOS has made opaque at full height fills with `glass-group`, as Add a word's deck search and picture description do, since `glass-plate` vanishes there.
- A row sheet grows with what it holds up to the room left on screen, the keyboard's height taken off while it is up, and past that it scrolls. Where more of it is out of sight, the edge softens into the sheet, as the top bar softens a page.
- A sheet glides to a new height rather than snapping ([motion.md](motion.md#timing)), so a screen never holds a sheet at a fixed height to hide a jump. Both libraries snap by default, so two patches apply each change through UIKit's `animateChanges`: `@expo/ui`'s for a `Sheet` growing with its content, and react-native-screens' for a `formSheet` route whose `sheetAllowedDetents` change while it is up, as the streak's do. The first presentation and Reduce Motion apply at once, and so does a `Sheet` while the keyboard moves. A `Sheet` with `more` takes its detents from the `presentationDetents` modifier and still snaps, so its peek height stays fixed while it is open.
- A row sheet's groups fill with `glass-group`, a faint ink tint in the light room and the glass plate's white at night, with no border, so they still read when iOS turns the sheet opaque at full height.

## After an action

The phone keeps the web's rule ([overlays.md](overlays.md)): what can be undone happens at once and offers Undo, and a question comes only before what cannot. Archiving a card or a deck, moving a deck between series and restoring from Archived never ask. Leaving a shared deck and deleting a series ask in iOS's alert, the safe button named for what stays and the destructive one last.

- What just happened shows in a toast: a 44 pt pill of smoked glass, iOS's compact size so it never takes over the screen under it, the text colour at 90% or more over a blur so Undo keeps 4.5:1 over any screen, 12 pt above whatever is pinned to the screen's foot: the tab bar, the selection bar, a review's grades or its end screen's buttons, or the safe area when nothing is. A control pinned to the foot wraps itself in `ToastFloor`, which measures its own top edge, so no screen sets a toast's height by hand; a screen iOS presents over the root, such as the review, provides its `ToastScope` and mounts its own `ToastHost`. It holds one sentence on at most two lines, at most one action in amber, usually Undo, and an icon. It stays ten seconds with an action and five without, as the web's does (`ACTION_TIMEOUT` in its `toast.tsx`), holds while touched, swipes away down or to the side, and stacks as the web's does. At night it is a light pill on the dark room. VoiceOver announces it as it appears, with `AccessibilityInfo.announceForAccessibility` since iOS has no live regions.
- A toast follows the learner off the screen the action closed, so archiving a deck lands on Library with its Undo.
- Where the screen itself is the answer, it changes in place instead: Explore's Add settles into In Library and Archived keeps a restored row with Undo. A sheet that stays open for more, such as Add a word, hosts its own toasts over its chips, since the app's sit under the sheet.
- An error is a toast with a red icon, named for what failed ("Couldn’t move “Verbi”."), and its action retries when retrying can help. A field that will not take its value says so under the field. iOS's alert is kept for a question the learner must answer, such as signing out with work not yet sent.
- A write that queues offline says once that it is saved on this phone, where Saved would show. A row that needs a connection is dimmed with Needs a connection as its detail before it is tapped: the label and icon fade, and the detail keeps its contrast.

## Menus

Commands from a button are iOS's own menu, `ActionMenu`: it grows out of its button in Liquid Glass, a drag through it picks, and VoiceOver reads it as a menu. The bar's ⋯ and Library's plus are `bar` and `primary` glass buttons; a list row's ⋯ is a bare `row` glyph. Its rows run from the most to the least used, and a removal (Archive, Leave, Remove) sits last in a group of its own, which iOS draws red. The same thing has the same rows in the same order wherever its menu opens, and the same job has one label everywhere. iOS draws the menu, so each Lucide icon in `ui/icons.tsx` is also a custom SF Symbol: `pnpm --filter @lymi/mobile symbols` (with SwiftDraw installed) writes them to `assets/symbols`, a config plugin copies them into the app at prebuild, and `pnpm check` fails when an icon has no symbol or a symbol no icon. A new icon therefore needs that command and a native build. A menu has no title when the thing it acts on stays in view.

A sheet takes over from the menu when a choice needs a second line, a long or searched list, or editing; when the trigger is the content itself, as a revealed card in review is; or when it is a filter.

A list row also opens iOS's context menu on a long press, through `RowMenu`: Library's and Today's deck rows, a card in a deck and in search, a section's heading, and a deck in Explore. Its rows are the ones that thing's ⋯ or page offers, in the same order, and only those the learner may take: a member's card has no Edit, Move or Archive. Its icons are the same custom symbols. Where it helps, the row lifts a preview: a deck's page, an Explore deck's page, a card's revealed side, sized to what the card holds. Each menu row is also a VoiceOver action on the row, since a long press is never found by ear, and a tap on the preview opens what it shows. Archived rows have none, since Restore already sits on the row.

## Add a word and Edit card

Adding and editing a card share one form. Add a word composes a card the way Linear composes an issue: a Liquid Glass sheet with close, the deck and Add as glass in its top corners, then the term, large with the keyboard already up, and the meaning. Each field is its name in small text over a line with no box, so a filled line still says what it holds and the empty one says what goes in it; all wrap as they fill. Return in the term goes to the meaning, Return in the meaning adds, and Return in the notes starts a new line.

The card's other text fields, pronunciation, example, notes, memory hook and source, show only once they hold something. While empty, each is a chip over the keyboard, after the AI's fill, and a tap adds that field after the fields already shown, with the keyboard on it, so a field appears where the learner is looking and a learner who never uses a field never sees it. It arrives as a plate does, the form scrolls to keep it in view, and it stays in view as it grows. Every chip looks the same whether it holds a value or not; its label is the value, and a chosen picture shows small in place of its icon. Only AI's chip takes a colour, since it is a switch. The card page keeps the fixed order. The chips run from the one used last on this phone, read once as the form opens so none moves under the finger; AI's fill stays first.

The deck, the section when the deck has any, the picture, how it's asked and the tags are choices rather than text. The picture modes show once the card has a picture, or while one is still ticked, and a ticked one says it waits for a picture or its description ([ADR 0014](../../adr/0014-review-modes-use-explicit-cues-and-targets.md)). Tags use the same list as Select cards' Tags ([library-decks-and-cards.md](../library-decks-and-cards.md)): a field finds a tag or offers Create for a new name, the tag typed listed first and then those starting with it, and Return takes it; below, the card's tags, then the deck's, then the learner's others, each ticked on or off as a draft that ✓ keeps. A card at twenty tags offers only taking one off. The deck sheet searches past fifteen decks, as a `Select` does. The picture is taken or chosen in its sheet with a description that does not name the answer, and uploads once the card exists; one chosen offline is dropped with a line that says so. A description that names the term or the meaning says so under it, and Add or ✓ opens the picture's sheet rather than sending it. Autocorrect is off wherever the text is in the language being learned: the term, the example and the pronunciation.

Add stays dim until there is a term. The sheet stays for the next card: a toast over the chips says where the last one went, with Undo, or which deck already has it, and each add replaces the last one's. A picture that did not reach its card says so in a toast with Try again. A duplicate keeps the form with the term selected, as on the web. The deck, the AI choice, the picture until it is added, the source and the tags carry over.

Edit card is the same form over a card that exists, with close and an amber ✓ in place of Add, and the picture, how it's asked and the tags as chips that open the same sheets; the card's own picture can be described again, replaced or cleared; ✓ or Return in the last field saves, and close or a swipe down leaves the card as it was. It opens from ⋯ on the card page and from the card's menu in a review, and a tap on any field of the card page opens it with the keyboard on that field, or on Asked or Tags with that sheet open. A field the AI wrote keeps the AI's mark until it is changed, and is the learner's once saved. Only a deck's owner sees Edit card; a member reads the card.

Archive is in the same ⋯ and the same menu. On the card page archiving goes back to the deck with Undo in a toast; in a review the card leaves at once, with Undo in a toast over the grades. Archived, the last row of Library, lists archived decks and cards with Restore, and a restored row keeps its place with Undo until the page is left.

## Review

## Offline

The phone keeps its query cache in MMKV for 30 days, as long as an unused session lasts, and fetches every deck's cards, sections and draw once while online, as the web does ([ADR 0023](../../adr/0023-offline-writes-queue-on-the-device-and-replay-in-order.md)). A cold launch with no connection opens Today, Library and each deck as of the last sync, and a review runs on the kept draw. A refresh that fails keeps the last good copy on disk, and warming that the connection cut short picks up on reconnect and on return to the front. Each query is kept under its own key and a write stores only the queries whose data changed, so a library of thousands of cards is not written again on every grade. The cache is versioned by the shapes the screens read rather than by the app's version, so an update fetched online never empties the next launch offline; it belongs to one learner and one Worker, and is forgotten when either changes.

While a screen shows what was kept, one muted line with a cloud at its top says so and since when: "You’re offline. Showing your cards as of 14:05." It is not an error and asks nothing. Today counts what is due from the kept draw and this phone's grades, because the decks' counts miss everything since the last sync. With nothing kept yet, Today says it couldn't load and offers to try again. Coming back online says nothing: queued grades and writes send, the screens refresh, and the line goes.

Signing out asks first when grades or changes made offline have not landed, as the web does, then forgets this learner's cache, outboxes and Add a word's choices; the theme and the reminder stay with the phone.

## Sound

## Glass

Liquid Glass suits Lymi: it is a material that holds light, and the lantern is a light. The phone uses it for chrome that floats over content: the tab bar, the top bar's buttons and sheets refract what scrolls beneath them and morph as the learner moves. Tinted amber, glass marks the one standing action, as plain amber marks a press on the web. Where else tinted glass carries Lymi's light is open, to try on a device rather than in mockups. What the learner reads, such as the review card, stays on opaque plates. Reduce Transparency, and iOS before 26, get the web's opaque surfaces. The web keeps its opaque pill.

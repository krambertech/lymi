# The phone app

`apps/mobile` shares tokens, brand assets and motion presets from `packages/design`, along with the web’s component names, Onest and Lucide. This guide documents native layout and interaction differences. Lymi renders the persistent navigation, rows and icons; iOS provides sheets, share sheets, alerts, keyboard, permission prompts and action menus ([ADR 0029](../../adr/0029-the-phone-app-is-expo-react-native-sharing-core.md)). Menu icons use custom symbols generated from Lucide ([Menus](#menus)). [PRODUCT.md](../../../PRODUCT.md#surfaces) defines the phone’s role. The iOS app targets iPhone; on iPad it runs at iPhone size. Use the web app for a wide iPad layout with navigation from 768 px.

## Navigation

- The tabs are iOS's own tab bar, in Onest with Lucide icons: Today, Library, Explore and You, each an icon over its label. The chosen tab is amber on the system's lens, its icon filled in `amber-mark` and its label in `amber-text`, so the choice reads by shape as well as colour ([colour.md](colour.md#amber-means-act)); the rest stay outlined in iOS's monochrome style. `pnpm --filter @lymi/mobile tab-icons` draws both from Lucide. Tapping the current tab returns its stack to the top. Before iOS 26 it is the system's classic bar ([ADR 0034](../../adr/0034-the-phone-uses-the-system-tab-bar-and-captures-from-the-top-bar.md)).
- Pin the top bar and its glass action buttons. Tab titles collapse from large to inline at the start edge; page titles move into the centre over 96 pt of scroll with easing, while named Back becomes a round button. Long titles wrap in the page and fade into the bar as they scroll out of view. Library, decks, Sections and Explore support pull to refresh through `Screen`’s `onRefresh`. Blur and tint the bar only when content scrolls beneath it, using native iOS blur to retain the page’s background colour.
- **You** starts with photo, name and email. Tapping opens a sheet to rename or take, choose or remove a photo. Insights follows, with the month’s recall and thirty-day strip after the first review, then reminder time, language and theme, Activity, feedback and sign-out. You contains the settings shown in web Settings. App language and meaning language belong to the account; theme and reminders belong to the device.
- **Your data**, between Activity and feedback, contains Import, Export library, AI services, Apps and API keys ([ADR 0036](../../adr/0036-setup-runs-on-the-phone.md)). Export opens a sheet. AI services opens the account switch, three feature rows and privacy-policy link ([ADR 0039](../../adr/0039-the-phone-sends-nothing-to-ai-until-the-learner-allows-it.md)). The other entries push pages.
- **Apps** lists connected apps with Unverified beside unrecognized names, then Add an app chips with Custom MCP last. Its empty state shows the assistant marks, Connect your first app, a description and chips directly on the background. App pages follow the shared header, Settings, Activity, How it connects and Stop structure; iOS alerts confirm Stop ([app-pages.md](../app-pages.md)).
- **API keys** uses the web’s rows, excluding Yomitan’s key. A sheet’s ✓ creates a key and shows it once with Copy key. Revoking from ⋯ requires an iOS alert. A Docs group links to guides with descriptions. Publishing and deck-report links open the API reference and appear only for accounts with a published deck.
- **Delete account** is the final danger action below sign-out. Its page counts the data to erase, explains decks retained for others, offers Export library and confirms with an emailed code before signing out. Archived is the final entry in Library.
- Capture is an amber glass button at the end of every tab's top bar, beside the streak. A tap opens Add a word as a sheet from below; a long press grows iOS's menu out of it with Add a card, Smart add and New deck, so the tap stays one word's quickest way in. Explore has neither: its bar holds Filter and Search in one glass capsule, because finding a deck there is the job, and holds nothing while no deck is published, and a word is caught from any other tab.
- Library's bar holds what Library is for instead: search, then the amber plus, iOS's own menu growing out of it (Add a card, Smart add, New deck, New series, Import decks, which opens Import). The streak stays on Today.
- Search sits at the foot of the screen and rides on the keyboard, as iOS 26's own search does: a glass field and a round glass close, with a filter between them where the screen has one. The screen above it is empty until the first letters are typed, and a tap or drag that sends the keyboard away from an empty field closes the search. Every search on the phone uses `SearchBar`.
- A deck opens on one summary plate, the count to review beside the deck's size, its cards' states as one bar with their counts under it, then a full-width Review. Once Review scrolls under the bar, the tab bar's accessory takes over, showing how many and a play button as Podcasts shows what is playing; scrolled back, it goes. Before iOS 26 there is no accessory, so the deck's own Review is the only one.
- Under the plate sit Filter and Sort. With filters on, Filter is ink, as a chosen chip is, with how many are on in a `canvas` badge, and the filters themselves are read and removed in its sheet ([Sheets](#sheets)). While the list is filtered, the summary plate, its Review and the accessory still mean the whole deck; the filtered cards are reviewed by Select cards, Select all, then Review due or Review all in its Actions menu, as on the web.

## On Android

Android uses the same screens, tabs, copy and themes, with Material conventions for system navigation and controls. Platform differences belong in `src/ui` and `src/theme`, through `.android.tsx` files or shared constants, rather than branching in screens or data code.

- The tabs are Material's navigation bar in the web rail's colours: `rail` behind, every tab labelled, and the chosen tab on a `rail-chosen` pill with its icon filled and amber, as on iOS, never the wallpaper's colours.
- A page's back is a bare arrow, and its title docks at the start beside it as the page scrolls. A tab's bar keeps the streak and the amber capture.
- Back does what the screen's own close does: in a review it ends on Review done, and Welcome steps back through its questions.
- Android uses Material bottom sheets with the `sheet` background, Lymi row groups and Lymi’s grabber. Form sheets fill the window. Button actions use Lymi menus with the same icons as iOS; Android 12 and later add blur through `ui/blur-root.tsx`. Long-press actions use a sheet. All stacks share the setup in `ui/stack.tsx`.
- Android takes both screen edges for Back, so a swipe that mirrors iOS's edge swipe, such as taking back a grade, starts anywhere on the card (`ui/edge-swipe.ts`).
- Status-bar icons follow the app theme. The keyboard, time picker and permission prompts use system controls.

## How a screen arrives

| Motion | For | Leaves by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Push from the side | A page to read or work in: a deck, a card, a series, Insights, Activity, a deck in Explore | The named back, or a swipe from the start edge |
| Sheet from below | A task with an end: Add a word, New deck, Edit card, a choice list such as the daily goal, the card menu in a review, the streak | A swipe down, with detents where the task grows |
| Cover from below | A mode that takes the screen: a review | X, or pulling the top bar down |
| Fade in place | A mode that replaces the screen: Welcome and sign-in | Finishing, or Skip for now |

This is [overlays.md](overlays.md)'s rule in iOS's own navigation: from the side means back, from below means swipe down.

The phone streak opens as a peek sheet showing the current streak and today’s progress; expand it to see the month. On web touch layouts it instead opens as a full-screen page ([streak.md](../streak.md)). Before opening the goal sheet, expand the streak sheet to full height so the new sheet can stack over it. Return to peek height when the goal sheet closes. Apply a preset after dismissal, use − and + for a custom value, and explain when a connection is required.

## Components

The phone's components are in `apps/mobile/src/ui` under the web's names, and follow [forms.md](forms.md), [buttons.md](buttons.md) and [chips-and-marks.md](chips-and-marks.md). They differ in these ways:

- Every control is 44 pt, so a `md` button is 44 pt tall and `sm` reaches 44 pt through its hit area.
- `xl` is Today's Review button alone, 64 pt with a 20 pt label as on the web. Buttons that stack at the foot, such as the end of a review and Welcome's Continue, are `lg`, 48 pt, since three at 64 pt outweighed the count above them.
- `Sheet` uses iOS presentation, supports dragging and sizes to its content. Centre the title and description. `SheetHeading`, `SheetNote` and `SheetError` align 16 pt inside a panel; use their shared insets. Row groups are inset 16 pt from the sheet edge and separated by 12 pt, with full-width dividers and 52 pt rows. Reserve icon or check slots consistently within a group. Use red for removal, a check for selection, a muted chevron for navigation and `value` for a trailing count. Peek sheets expand to full height, where iOS makes the background opaque.
- `Select` uses sheet rows with optional descriptions and applies a choice after dismissal. For more than fifteen fixed options, use a searchable list: focus search on open, keep the list height fixed, highlight the first match for Return, dismiss the keyboard on drag and let the first tap select a row even with the keyboard open. [Sheets](#sheets) defines the presentation types.
- A sheet's own controls float on it as glass: a round 44 pt button in each top corner, close at the start and the sheet's action at the end, tinted amber, with a glass pill between them for what the sheet works in, such as the deck.
- Chips come in `sm` and `md`, and a state chip is one size wherever it shows, Forgotten included; the AI badge is the `sm` chip. A due count has one size. The web's `xs` chip, `lg` chip, `lg` due count and `xs` icon button have no phone use yet.
- `Switch` is drawn by Lymi, amber when on, so it sits in a form beside the other controls.
- Every error line is `FieldError` from `ui/field.tsx`: the alert icon centres on the text's first line however it wraps, and VoiceOver reads it as it appears. It starts 4 pt in, where `FieldDescription` and a form's other lines outside a plate start, or flush (`flush`) under a field drawn as a line with no box, as in Add a word. Biome refuses a hand-made one.
- Every spring comes from `@lymi/design`: `withSpring(value, spring(PRESET))`, or `springIn(new Builder(), PRESET)` for a layout animation. A single physics value such as `.damping(18)` keeps Reanimated 4's heavy defaults beside it and overshoots, so Biome refuses it.
- Tabular figures are for a figure on its own that changes in place or lines up in a column, such as a due count. A number inside a phrase, such as "21 cards", keeps Onest's own figures, or a 1 sits apart from its neighbours.
- React Native ignores `text-end` and `text-start`, so end-aligned text takes `TEXT_END` from `theme/type.ts`, and Biome refuses the classes.
- A card's own words, its term, reading and example, pass the card's language to `Text` as `lang`. React Native's text carries no language, so on a non-Japanese iPhone the system's fallback from Onest gives kanji that Chinese shares their Chinese forms; for a card whose language names a script face (`languageScript`), `Text` draws that script's runs in the first face of its `--face-<code>` token, Hiragino Sans for Japanese and PingFang SC or TC for simplified or traditional Chinese, and leaves the rest in Onest, as the web's `:lang()` rules do. A reading written in letters, as pinyin and Jyutping are, keeps a word space's room in all, an eighth of its size each side (`readingInset`), so a syllable wider than its characters never runs into the next. Android's fallback has no family to name, so it keeps the device's choice.
- A card's term is `TermText` and its example `ExampleText`, both from `ui/term-text.tsx`: they choose between drawing a reading and plain text, so no screen asks for a term's reading itself. Furigana is `Furigana` from `ui/furigana.tsx`. React Native has no ruby, so each run is a column with its reading over it, and the columns wrap: a run with a reading never breaks, plain kana and kanji break between characters, and a closing mark or small kana stays on the line before. Readings follow [type.md](type.md#languages-readings-and-stress)'s shared proportions, in points and scaled with Dynamic Type, so every line of a Japanese term stands a reading's height taller whether or not it has one. A hidden reading keeps its room and fades in at the reveal. VoiceOver hears the reading in place of the kanji while it shows, and the term as written while it is hidden. A list of Japanese terms keeps the room on rows without one (`room`), as the web's deck does. A term's forms flow after it as `tail` and wrap with it; a row cut at its lines ends in an ellipsis and keeps the ★ (`after`) past the cut. Text with no reading to draw stays one plain `Text`, and only a review cue, which passes `hidden`, sets up the fade. A Russian term whose pronunciation marks its stress is drawn as that marked form, за́мок, in the same plain `Text`; `hidden` draws the plain term instead, with no fade. `ExampleText` given the learner's Known pairs (`known`, from `useKnownReadings`) keeps the room of a reading over a word they know but draws nothing there, and VoiceOver hears that word as written.
- Use `DragList` from `ui/drag-list.tsx` for reordering. A trailing grip starts the drag, other rows shift to show the destination, and dropping saves. Each row also exposes Move up and Move down to VoiceOver. Sections in Edit and Series order use it.
- A bar that floats above the tab bar, such as the selection toolbar, goes through `useFloatingBar`: the root draws it, since only the root knows where the screen ends, it leaves when its screen loses focus, and a toast rises above it. It slides in rather than fading, because glass that starts transparent never draws its effect.
- Use `Screen`’s `list` for lists that can contain hundreds of rows. It mounts nearby rows only; memoization limits redraws to changed cards. Rows with native long-press menus are expensive to mount, so a full deck must not render at once.
- A stepper's − and + repeat while held (`IconButton`'s `repeat`) and dim at the bounds.
- A page's intro sentence is `Screen`'s `description`, held 8 pt under the large title. A page whose header holds its name passes `titleAt`, so the bar's title arrives as the name goes under the bar, and a page with its own `ground` colours its bar with it.

The native design screen at `lymi://design` shows component states, like `/design` on web. Each canvas has its own theme switch.

## Empty states

`EmptyState` in `ui/empty-state.tsx` is every place with nothing to show, following [empty-states.md](empty-states.md)'s rules in one component rather than the web's several. Why the place is empty picks the icon, and where it sits picks the size.

- **Kinds:** `start` for nothing made yet, `noMatch` for a search or filter that matched nothing, `failed` for a read that failed, and `offline` for a place that needs a connection the phone does not have. Only `failed` is red; being offline is no failure.
- **Sizes:** `page` when the whole screen is empty, large and centred in the room the bars leave, with a primary button; `inline` inside a plate already on the screen, which is its surface, with a small secondary button. It never brings a plate of its own.
- The icon sits in a soft disc, never the lantern, and the title names what is missing: "No decks match “map”", not "Nothing here". Still loading is a skeleton, never an empty state.

## Sheets

Sheets present from the bottom and support swipe dismissal. Choose their type from the task’s content; the type determines presentation and background fills:

| Kind | For | Built with | Surface | Fills |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Row sheet | A choice or a one-field edit over the screen: a `Select`, Filter (which saves), a deck's name, a section's name, the learner's name and photo, the hint | `Sheet`, inside the screen | iOS's glass, untinted, at the height of what it holds | On-glass |
| Peek sheet | A screen to glance at that drags up for more: the streak, the cards an Activity row wrote | `Sheet` with `more`, or a route presented as `formSheet` with detents | Glass while it peeks; iOS makes it opaque at full height | On-glass |
| Short form | A form that fits on one screen and is done in seconds: Add a word, Edit card, New deck, New series, Share deck | A route presented as `formSheet`. Add a word, Edit card and New deck open at full height, so iOS draws them opaque; New series sizes to its fields and Share deck peeks | Glass below full height; opaque at full height | On-glass, reading on either |
| Long form | A form that scrolls or holds long text: feedback, Choose decks, Smart add | A route presented as `formSheet` at full height | Opaque, the sheet colour | The room's own, since on-glass fills vanish against an opaque sheet |

- Glass wherever the task is quick; opaque where the learner reads or types at length. A form that grows several sections or a long text field becomes a long form. iOS draws any sheet opaque at full height, so a short form stays glass only while it opens below full height.
- When glass is disabled, Reduce Transparency is enabled or iOS is older than 26, use the opaque sheet background and normal theme fills for groups. Check each sheet in this fallback state.
- Use a row sheet for a one-field edit. Its title names the field, so omit a duplicate label. Use `bg-glass-plate`, 44 pt for one line, and grow with content. Multiline fields use `MULTILINE` from `theme/type.ts` for top alignment. Start paragraph-length fields, such as AI instructions, with a taller input.
- `Sheet` owns the header: a centred `text-md` title and optional description. Forms and multi-select lists use `save` for close at the start and an amber ✓ at the end. ✓ and the keyboard’s done key save; close and swipe cancel. Immediate-choice lists have a title without action buttons and dismiss on selection or swipe. Command sheets and content-led peeks may omit a header. Use the header action rather than a full-width save button below a field. The design screen demonstrates each header state.
- `Sheet`’s optional description is centred below the title and limited to two lines in every language. Explain an effect, rule or connection requirement that the rows cannot convey. Omit instructions that merely describe how to use the rows. Put group-specific guidance in `SheetNote`. Leave 20 pt between the header and first group.
- Use a stepper for numeric choices such as a custom daily goal. It preserves the sheet height and avoids opening a number keyboard with another Done action.
- A form keeps its fields as labelled lines with no box ([Add a word](#add-a-word-and-edit-card)); only a card's term is set large. Once something is typed, a swipe asks before it throws the draft away.
- One of a list runs once the sheet has gone, everywhere. Several of a list are ticked as a draft that ✓ saves, as Filter, a card's tags, how it's asked and the fields AI fills do; close and a swipe leave them as they were.
- Filters sit in one sheet that saves, titled Filter, with how many cards the drafted filters keep centred under the title as `Sheet`'s `subtitle`; its ✓ applies them. Every filter is a `SheetHeading` over one plate of rows whose labels share a line: State's checkboxes, Due's Any time, Today and This week as a radio, each with the cards it keeps, then Section and Tags, each shown only when the deck has some, as one row each, its icon in the slot and its label naming what is picked or Any section and Any tag, that opens its list on a second level of the same sheet. There a back chevron takes close's place, the list keeps the first level's height and scrolls within it, since iOS snaps a sheet to a new height rather than gliding, and it fades in from the side the learner is going toward; past fifteen rows a search field leads, as a long `Select`'s does. Under the last group, Reset filters is a centred text action with its icon, set apart from the sections because it acts on the whole sheet, shown once anything is drafted. Filter is the one sheet with a second level.
- A row sheet never opens a sheet, the camera or the photo library over itself, and never moves to another screen while it is open. A row that needs one closes the sheet and acts once it has gone. Add a word's picture sheet is the exception: iOS's camera and photo picker open over it, so the picture comes back to the sheet where it is described. A form may open a row sheet over itself, which stacks on it; a part-height sheet cannot, because iOS pushes it out of view under the new one, so a part-height sheet first rises to full height and the new sheet stacks over it, as the streak does for its daily goal.
- A `formSheet` route lays out at most a header and one scroll view. A header beside the scroll view takes `collapsable={false}`, or iOS lays the scroll view over it.
- Every row sheet has a title in its header. A peek sheet and a form carry their controls as glass in the top corners, as a sheet that saves does.
- A form's header is one shared row: close at the start, named Cancel when leaving discards something and Close when it does not; its name centred at `text-md`, or a pill for what it works in, such as Add a word's deck; the one action at the end. Nothing under it repeats the name as a large title; a line saying what the sheet is for comes first in the content.
- A field or search box on a sheet iOS has made opaque at full height fills with `glass-group`, as Add a word's deck search and picture description do, since `glass-plate` vanishes there.
- A row sheet grows to the available screen height, subtracting the keyboard while visible, then scrolls. Fade the content edge only where more content is outside the visible area.
- Animate content-height and `sheetAllowedDetents` changes through UIKit’s `animateChanges`. Patches cover `@expo/ui`’s `Sheet` and react-native-screens’ `formSheet` routes. Do not mask snapping with a fixed height. Initial presentation, Reduce Motion and a `Sheet` following a moving keyboard apply changes immediately. A `Sheet` with `more` uses `presentationDetents` and keeps its peek height fixed while open ([motion.md](motion.md#timing)).
- A row sheet's groups fill with `glass-group`, a faint ink tint in light theme and the glass plate's white in dark theme, with no border, so they still read when iOS turns the sheet opaque at full height.

## After an action

The phone keeps the web's rule ([overlays.md](overlays.md)): what can be undone happens at once and offers Undo, and a question comes only before what cannot. Archiving a card or a deck, moving a deck between series and restoring from Archived never ask. Leaving a shared deck and deleting a series ask in iOS's alert, the safe button named for what stays and the destructive one last.

- Toasts are 44 pt glass capsules with at least 90% text-colour opacity over blur, preserving 4.5:1 text contrast. Position them 12 pt above the bottommost controls or safe area. `ToastFloor` measures this clearance; screens presented above the root provide `ToastScope` and `ToastHost`. Use one sentence on at most two lines, one optional amber action, usually Undo, and an icon. Display for ten seconds with an action or five without (`ACTION_TIMEOUT`); pause while touched, support downward or sideways dismissal and stack like web toasts. Dark-theme toasts use a light fill. Announce them through `AccessibilityInfo.announceForAccessibility`.
- A toast follows the learner off the screen the action closed, so archiving a deck lands on Library with its Undo.
- Where the screen itself is the answer, it changes in place instead: Explore's Add settles into In Library and Archived keeps a restored row with Undo. A sheet that stays open for more, such as Add a word, hosts its own toasts over its chips, since the app's sit under the sheet.
- An error is a toast with a red icon, named for what failed ("Couldn’t move “Verbi”."), and its action retries when retrying can help. A field that will not take its value says so under the field. iOS's alert is kept for a question the learner must answer, such as signing out with work not yet sent.
- A write that queues offline says once that it is saved on this phone, where Saved would show. A row that needs a connection is dimmed with Needs a connection as its detail before it is tapped: the label and icon fade, and the detail keeps its contrast.

## Menus

Commands from a button are iOS's own menu, `ActionMenu`: it grows out of its button in Liquid Glass, a drag through it picks, and VoiceOver reads it as a menu. The bar's ⋯ and Library's plus are `bar` and `primary` glass buttons; a list row's ⋯ is a bare `row` glyph. Its rows run from the most to the least used, and a removal (Archive, Leave, Remove) sits last in a group of its own, which iOS draws red. The same thing has the same rows in the same order wherever its menu opens, and the same job has one label everywhere. iOS draws the menu, so each Lucide icon in `ui/icons.tsx` is also a custom SF Symbol: `pnpm --filter @lymi/mobile symbols` (with SwiftDraw installed) writes them to `assets/symbols`, a config plugin copies them into the app at prebuild, and `pnpm check` fails when an icon has no symbol or a symbol no icon. A new icon therefore needs that command and a native build. A menu has no title when the thing it acts on stays in view.

A sheet takes over from the menu when a choice needs a second line, a long or searched list, or editing; when the trigger is the content itself, as a revealed card in review is; or when it is a filter.

A list row also opens iOS's context menu on a long press, through `RowMenu`: Library's and Today's deck rows, a card in a deck and in search, a section's heading, and a deck in Explore. Its rows are the ones that thing's ⋯ or page offers, in the same order, and only those the learner may take: a member's card has no Edit, Move or Archive. Its icons are the same custom symbols. Where it helps, the row lifts a preview: a deck's page, an Explore deck's page, a card's revealed side, sized to what the card holds. Each menu row is also a VoiceOver action on the row, since a long press is never found by ear, and a tap on the preview opens what it shows. Archived rows have none, since Restore already sits on the row.

## Add a word and Edit card

Adding and editing use the same form. Add a word opens a sheet with close, deck and Add in its header, then a large term field and meaning with the keyboard open. Fields have small labels above unboxed lines and wrap as needed. Return moves from term to meaning, submits from meaning and inserts a line break in notes.

The card's other text fields, pronunciation, example, notes, hint and source, show only once they hold something. While empty, each is a chip over the keyboard, after the AI's fill, and a tap adds that field after the fields already shown, with the keyboard on it, so a field appears where the learner is looking and a learner who never uses a field never sees it. It arrives as a plate does, the form scrolls to keep it in view, and it stays in view as it grows. Every chip looks the same whether it holds a value or not; its label is the value, and a chosen picture shows small in place of its icon. Only AI's chip takes a colour, since it is a switch. The example's translation has no chip and no name of its own: once the example holds text, a block indented under it behind a 2 pt `edge` hairline holds its own small Translation name, with the AI's mark beside it, over the translation at the fields' size in `ink-secondary`, its hint in `ink-faint`, so it reads as part of the example and still as text to edit. With nothing typed it says the AI translates it after the save where the deck lets it, and otherwise offers to add one. The card page keeps the fixed order. The chips run from the one used last on this phone, read once as the form opens so none moves under the finger; AI's fill stays first.

The deck, the section when the deck has any, the picture, how it's asked and the tags are choices rather than text. Each opens from its chip, the deck from its pill, as a row sheet over the form, sized to what it holds. The keyboard leaves while the form holds its place, and the chips sink and fade as the sheet rises; once the sheet has gone, the keyboard returns on the term and the chips rise back one after another ([motion.md](motion.md)). A deck or a section is chosen with a tap, which closes the sheet; how it's asked is the deck's modes or modes ticked here, kept with ✓; unticking the last one goes back to the deck's. The picture modes show once the card has a picture, or while one is still ticked, and a ticked one says it waits for a picture or its description ([ADR 0014](../../adr/0014-review-modes-use-explicit-cues-and-targets.md)). Tags use the same list as Select cards' Tags ([library-decks-and-cards.md](../library-decks-and-cards.md)): a field finds a tag or offers Create for a new name, the tag typed listed first and then those starting with it, and Return takes it; below, the card's tags, then the deck's, then the learner's others, each ticked on or off as a draft that ✓ keeps. A card at twenty tags offers only taking one off. The deck sheet searches past fifteen decks, as a `Select` does. The picture is taken or chosen in its sheet with a description that does not name the answer, and uploads once the card exists; one chosen offline is dropped with a line that says so. A description that names the term or the meaning says so under it, and Add or ✓ opens the picture's sheet rather than sending it. Autocorrect is off wherever the text is in the language being learned: the term, the example and the pronunciation.

Add stays dim until there is a term. The sheet stays for the next card: a toast over the chips says where the last one went, with Undo, or which deck already has it, and each add replaces the last one's. A picture that did not reach its card says so in a toast with Try again. A duplicate keeps the form with the term selected, as on the web. The deck, the AI choice, the picture until it is added, the source and the tags carry over.

Edit card is the same form over a card that exists, with close and an amber ✓ in place of Add, and the picture, how it's asked and the tags as chips that open the same sheets; the card's own picture can be described again, replaced or cleared; ✓ or Return in the last field saves, and close or a swipe down leaves the card as it was. It opens from ⋯ on the card page and from the card's menu in a review, and a tap on any field of the card page opens it with the keyboard on that field, or on Asked or Tags with that sheet open. A field the AI wrote keeps the AI's mark until it is changed, and is the learner's once saved. Only a deck's owner sees Edit card; a member reads the card.

Offer Enrich only in the card page’s ⋯ under the shared [enrichment rules](../library-decks-and-cards.md). Omit it from long-press and review menus and while enrichment is running, since iOS menus do not show disabled rows. Ask for AI permission when needed. Show field shimmers, announce start and outcome through VoiceOver, and use one failure message below the fields, including translation failures.

Archive is in the same ⋯ and the same menu. On the card page archiving goes back to the deck with Undo in a toast; in a review the card leaves at once, with Undo in a toast over the grades. The steps are measured before the reveal, so the question keeps its size when the answer appears. The question is never so large its longest word must break, and grows with the text size up to 1.4×; the answer grows at most 1.3×, so it always stays below the question. The speaker follows the answer's last word and wraps with it, centred on the line at the size it is drawn, and stands whole at every text size. The example's translation takes its own line under it in `ink-muted`, as on the web. ## Offline

The phone keeps its query cache in MMKV for 30 days, as long as an unused session lasts, and fetches every deck's cards, sections and draw once while online, as the web does ([ADR 0023](../../adr/0023-offline-writes-queue-on-the-device-and-replay-in-order.md)). A cold launch with no connection opens Today, Library and each deck as of the last sync, and a review runs on the kept draw. A refresh that fails keeps the last good copy on disk, and warming that the connection cut short picks up on reconnect and on return to the front. Each query is kept under its own key and a write stores only the queries whose data changed, so a library of thousands of cards is not written again on every grade. The cache is versioned by the shapes the screens read rather than by the app's version, so an update fetched online never empties the next launch offline; it belongs to one learner and one Worker, and is forgotten when either changes.

While a screen shows what was kept, one muted line with a cloud at its top says so and since when: "You’re offline. Showing your cards as of 14:05." It is not an error and asks nothing. Today counts what is due from the kept draw and this phone's grades, because the decks' counts miss everything since the last sync. With nothing kept yet, Today says it couldn't load and offers to try again. Coming back online says nothing: queued grades and writes send, the screens refresh, and the line goes.

Signing out asks first when grades or changes made offline have not landed, as the web does, then forgets this learner's cache, outboxes and Add a word's choices; the theme and the reminder stay with the phone.
